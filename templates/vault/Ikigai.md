# Ikigai

Mieko Kamiya, *Ikigai ni tsuite* (1966) — not the four-circle diagram, which is
a Spanish purpose chart from 2011 that was relabelled in 2014 and is not
Japanese. Two distinctions carry the whole file:

- **ikigai-kan** is the felt sense that life is worth living. It moves daily,
  and it is the 1–5 number in each daily note.
- **ikigai** is the *sources* — the things themselves. They change over years.

## Ikigai-kan

One paragraph, in your words: what it is actually like on a day when life feels
worth living. Not what you want it to be like. Rewrite it quarterly.

## How I score ikigai-kan

The 1-5 you write in each daily note. Anchor each number to a **remembered day**,
not an adjective — "good" and "fine" drift, a specific Tuesday does not. Written
once, re-read whenever the number is hard to pick.

Everything the ikigai evidence pass does is compare these numbers to each other,
so a 3 in August has to mean what a 3 in November means. Until all five are
filled in, the daily readings are not comparable and the count toward the
sixty-day threshold is measuring nothing.

## Sources

Things that are a source of ikigai right now. Concrete, present tense, not
aspirations.

Give each one a `#goal` tag. That is what lets Lumis count the days you actually
touched it and compare your felt sense on those days against your baseline. A
source with no tag is a claim that can never be wrong, which makes it useless
here — and Lumis will tell you so rather than quietly skipping it.

## The seven needs

Kamiya's seven, in her order. Life satisfaction is the one she calls most basic.
Fill in what each means *for you* — the table is a definition, not a score.

Scores live in `Life/Reviews/Ikigai YYYY-Qn.md`, one note per quarter, so that
seven time series accumulate. A single number is not a finding. A need sitting
at 2 for three quarters is.
